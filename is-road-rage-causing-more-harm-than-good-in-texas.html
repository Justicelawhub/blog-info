Frequently Asked Questions

1. What's the difference between road rage and aggressive driving?

Aggressive driving usually means unsafe behavior such as tailgating, speeding, weaving or running lights. Road rage generally describes more intense, hostile behavior by an angry driver, which can involve confrontation. Legal definitions can vary, so an attorney can explain how they apply to a specific situation.

2. What should I do if another driver is road raging at me?

Stay calm, avoid eye contact and gestures, and don't try to retaliate. Create distance if you can, and don't drive home or pull over somewhere isolated. Head to a busy public place, and call 911 if you feel threatened.

3. What should I do after a road rage crash in Texas?

Move to safety if you can and call 911. Get medical attention, even for injuries that seem minor. Exchange information, photograph the scene and vehicles, and collect witness contact details. Avoid arguing with the other driver, and consider speaking with an attorney before giving detailed statements.

4. Can I sue after a road rage crash in Texas?

It may be possible to pursue a claim, depending on the facts, the evidence, your injuries and the insurance involved. Time limits apply to legal claims, so it's wise to speak with a licensed Texas attorney soon after a crash.

5. Why does documentation matter after a crash?

Photos, video or dashcam footage, witness details, the police report and medical records can all help show what happened. Gathering them early, while details are fresh, gives you a clearer record to work from later.
